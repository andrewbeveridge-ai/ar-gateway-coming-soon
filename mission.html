The mission

The people with the most to gain are being taught the least.

Gateway exists to close that gap — starting with anyone willing to spend ten minutes learning to ask better.

The gap

AI is the biggest change to how ordinary work gets done in a generation. The people best positioned to use it are the ones who already work in tech — they have the vocabulary, the confidence and the coworkers to ask.

Everyone else gets told to "just try it." They type a question, get a generic answer back, quietly conclude it isn't for them, and go back to doing the work the long way. That's not a talent gap. It's a teaching gap.

Meanwhile the same tools are quietly reshaping who gets hired, who gets promoted and whose job survives the next five years. A gap in access to good teaching turns into a gap in income.

Nobody should lose out on this because the instructions were written for somebody else.

What we're actually doing about it

Not a manifesto — three rules that decide what gets published here.

01

The foundation stays free

The Starter Kit, the daily content, the prompts, the tool guides. Not a trial, not a teaser with the good part removed. Free, and it stays that way.

02

Plain English, always

If something needs jargon to sound impressive, it gets rewritten. Anyone who can use a phone can use what's taught here.

03

Teach the skill, not the dependency

Even the paid products end with you able to build the thing yourself. The goal is that you stop needing us.

No gatekeeping, in practice

It's easy to say. Here's what it costs us to mean it:

Full prompts, every time. No "DM me for the full version." If a technique gets taught here, it gets taught completely.

Honest tool comparisons. Including when the free plan beats the paid one, and when the answer is "you don't need to pay for anything yet." There are no affiliate deals with any AI company, so there's nothing pulling the recommendations.

Uncertainty gets labeled. Pricing changes monthly, platform rules change without warning, and models get capabilities they didn't have last quarter. When something is unverified, it says so rather than being dressed up as fact.

Where this goes

Right now Gateway is one person, a daily posting habit and a free kit. The direction it's pointed, though, is specific:

Now

Teach the basics in public, daily. Build the free library out until there's a real answer for whatever someone is stuck on.

Next

Go deep on the moments that change someone's income — getting hired first, then side income, everyday work, career switchers and students.

Eventually

Get this to the people who never see it at all: workforce programs, community organizations, adult learners, people retraining after a career ends. That's the long-term aim, and it's the reason the free tier isn't a marketing tactic.
