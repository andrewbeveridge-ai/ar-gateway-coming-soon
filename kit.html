The "Interview Me First"

Any task

The single biggest upgrade. Instead of guessing, the AI pulls the context out of you.

I want help with [the task — e.g. planning my kid's birthday / writing a LinkedIn post / fixing my budget]. Before you give me anything, ask me up to 5 questions, one at a time, that you need answered to do this really well. After my last answer, give me your best version.
02

Explain It Like I'm New

Learning

Learn anything — a new job skill, a medical bill, a lease — without feeling dumb.

Explain [topic] to me like I'm smart but completely new to it. Use one everyday analogy, then give me the 3 things that actually matter and the 1 mistake beginners make. Finish with a 3-question quiz so I know I've got it.
03

The Resume Bullet Upgrade

Job search

Turns "responsible for" duties into results a hiring manager actually notices.

Act as a hiring manager for [target job title]. Here are bullets from my resume: [paste bullets]. Rewrite each one to lead with a strong verb and show a result. Where a number would help and I haven't given one, ask me for it — don't invent it. Show before and after side by side.
04

The Job Posting Decoder

Job search

See what a posting is really asking for — and exactly where you match.

Here's a job posting: [paste posting]. And here's my resume: [paste resume]. Give me: (1) the 5 things this employer cares about most, in plain English, (2) where I'm a strong match, with proof from my resume, (3) my honest gaps and how I could address each one in my application.
05

The Practice Interviewer

Job search

Rehearse out loud with a patient interviewer who gives real feedback.

Act as an interviewer for a [job title] role at a [type of company]. Ask me one interview question at a time — mix of behavioral and role-specific. After each answer, tell me what worked, what was weak, and show me a stronger version using my own details. Don't move to the next question until I say "next."
06

The Hard Email

Everyday work

For the message you've been putting off: asking for a raise, pushing back, saying no.

Help me write an email to [who — e.g. my manager / a landlord / a client]. Situation: [what happened]. What I want: [the outcome]. Give me 2 versions — one direct, one softer — each under 150 words. Sound like a calm, confident person, not a corporate robot.
07

Mess to Action List

Everyday work

Meeting notes, voice memos, a chaotic group chat — turned into who does what by when.

Below are my messy notes from [a meeting / a phone call / my brain dump]. Turn them into: (1) a 3-sentence summary, (2) a list of action items with who owns each and any deadline mentioned, (3) open questions nobody answered. If something's unclear, flag it instead of guessing. Notes: [paste]
08

Plan My Week (For Real)

Life

A schedule built around your actual energy and obligations, not an ideal robot's.

Help me plan next week. Fixed commitments: [work hours, kids' pickups, appointments]. Things I need to get done: [list]. I have the most energy [mornings / evenings]. Build a realistic day-by-day plan with buffer time, put the hardest task where my energy is highest, and tell me what to drop if the week goes sideways.
09

The Decision Helper

Life

Stuck between options? Get the angles you haven't considered — without it deciding for you.

I'm deciding between [option A] and [option B]. What matters most to me: [e.g. money, time with family, stability]. Lay out the pros, cons, and hidden costs of each, then tell me what would have to be true for each option to be the right call. Ask me anything you need to know. Don't pick for me — help me see it clearly.
10

The Honest Critic

Any task

AI is polite by default. This makes it tell you what's actually wrong — then fix it.

Here's something I wrote: [paste — resume, post, email, essay]. Its goal is [what it needs to do]. Be a tough but fair editor: list the 3 biggest problems in order of importance, explain why each one hurts, then give me a revised version that fixes them. Don't soften the feedback.
