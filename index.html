The problem

Most people never get past question one.

They type a short question, get a generic answer, and quietly decide AI is overrated. Three habits change that — none of them technical.

I.

Brief it like a person

Role, context, task, format. The briefing you’d give a sharp new colleague on their first day.

II.

Treat the first answer as a draft

The quality arrives in the second and third message. “Shorter.” “More specific.” “Sound more like me.”

III.

Let it ask you

Five questions from the AI will beat an hour of you trying to describe everything perfectly up front.

What most people type
write a follow up email after my interview

What comes back: a stiff template that opens with “I am writing to express my continued interest,” says nothing, and gives no one a reason to reply.

What the formula gets you
Act as a hiring manager who reads 40 of these a week. Context: I interviewed Tuesday for an office coordinator role at a dental practice. The office manager said they’re drowning in scheduling errors since two people left, and I told her about the intake system I rebuilt at my last job. Task: write a short follow-up that reminds her of that conversation and makes her picture me solving it. Format: under 120 words, warm and direct, no clichés.

What comes back: an email about her scheduling problem and your fix for it — the kind a hiring manager actually answers.

Role, context, task, format. It works on everything, not just job hunting — and the kit has ten prompts already built this way.
