The problem

Most people never get past question one.

They type a short question, get a generic answer, and quietly decide AI is overrated. Three habits change that — none of them technical.

I.

Tell it what you need, like you would a person

Who it should act as, what’s going on, what you want and how you want it — just like explaining a job to a helpful new coworker.

II.

Treat the first answer as a draft

The quality arrives in the second and third message. “Shorter.” “More specific.” “Sound more like me.”

III.

Let it ask you

Five questions from the AI will beat an hour of you trying to describe everything perfectly up front.

What most people type
help me make a budget

What comes back: the generic 50/30/20 rule and a blank template — nothing about your actual life.

What the formula gets you
Act as a patient financial coach for regular people. Context: I take home $3,400 a month, rent is $1,250, I have a $310 car payment, and I always seem to run out of money the last week of the month. Task: build me a simple monthly budget and find $200 I could save. Format: a short table, then three specific changes, starting with the easiest.

What comes back: a budget built around your real paycheck and bills, three changes you could make this week — and a real $200 a month to save.

Role, context, task, format. It works on everything — meals, emails, budgets, job hunting — and the kit has ten prompts already built this way.
